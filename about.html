The Self-Learner Trait

I believe the most important trait of any great engineer is the ability — and the hunger — to teach yourself. It's the skill that multiplies every other.

When I started my BSc at SUNY Oswego in 2019, AI wasn't part of the curriculum. Rather than wait for formal instruction, I went through Coursera, Udacity, papers, and open-source code on my own — building my ML foundation in parallel with my degree. That habit of not waiting for permission to learn has defined my career ever since.

The same approach extends into keeping up with the field: new architectures, emerging frameworks, research directions I find interesting — I read, implement, and experiment without needing it to be assigned. And I apply the same mindset to fitness: tracking my own training, nutrition, and recovery rather than blindly following templates. It's the same feedback loop — observe, adjust, iterate.

I got into one of Vietnam's most selective universities on my own, earned scholarships abroad on my own, and taught myself a field that didn't exist in my curriculum — on my own. Not because I had to, but because I genuinely believe that ownership of your own learning is what separates engineers who grow from those who plateau.
